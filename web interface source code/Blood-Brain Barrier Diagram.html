The Blood-Brain Barrier (BBB)

A highly selective, semipermeable cellular interface that separates the circulating blood from the brain and extracellular fluid in the central nervous system (CNS). It is essential for maintaining cerebral homeostasis and protecting neural tissue from pathogens and neurotoxins.

Fig 1. Macro Structure

The BBB exists along all capillaries within the CNS, representing a total surface area of roughly 12-20 m², forming a highly regulated biochemical vault.

Endothelial Cells

The fundamental building blocks of the BBB. Unlike peripheral capillaries, cerebral endothelial cells lack fenestrations (pores), strictly limiting pinocytosis and passive diffusion.

Tight Junctions

Protein complexes (claudins, occludins) that completely seal the paracellular clefts between adjacent endothelial cells. This forces all traffic to utilize transcellular pathways.

Astrocytes & Pericytes

Astrocytic endfeet ensheath over 90% of the capillary, providing biochemical support. Pericytes embedded in the basement membrane regulate capillary blood flow and barrier permeability.

Selective Transport

Small lipophilic molecules (O₂, CO₂) diffuse passively. Crucial water-soluble nutrients (like glucose) require specific carrier-mediated transport proteins (e.g., GLUT1) to cross the membrane.
