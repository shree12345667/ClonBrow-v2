X-Ray Density Lab

Understand how material density affects X-ray attenuation. High-density materials block X-rays (causing shadows), while low-density materials let them pass.

Beer-Lambert Law (Attenuation)
I = I₀ · e-μx
I = Transmitted Intensity
I₀ = Initial X-Ray Intensity
μ = Linear Attenuation Coefficient (Density)
Higher voltage increases photon energy, allowing rays to penetrate denser materials.
Film Exposure: Black (Radiolucent)
Air has extremely low density. Most X-ray photons pass through and strike the detector, fully exposing the film and turning it black.
Counter-Intuitive Fact: X-ray film starts white! Photons hitting the film cause a chemical reaction that turns it black. Therefore, bones (which block photons) leave a white "shadow" on the film.
