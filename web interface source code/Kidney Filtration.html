Renal Physiology Ultrafiltration

The Glomerular Filtration Barrier

Blood is filtered under high pressure across a specialized three-layered sieve. This process separates metabolic waste and small solutes from essential blood cells and large proteins.

Microscopic Cross-Section

Hydrostatic Pressure

1. The Glomerulus

A network of high-pressure capillaries. Unlike other beds, blood enters via a wide afferent arteriole and exits a narrower efferent arteriole, creating the Hydrostatic Pressure required for filtration.

2. Filtration Barrier (The Sieve)

Consists of fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, and podocyte slit diaphragms. It acts as a size and charge-selective filter, blocking molecules larger than 8nm (like Albumin).

3. Bowman's Capsule

The funnel-shaped start of the nephron. It collects the Ultrafiltrate—a fluid nearly identical to plasma but lacking proteins—and directs it toward the Proximal Convoluted Tubule.
