Mechanisms of Filtration

The kidney filters 180 liters of blood a day through millions of these microscopic sieves.

Size Exclusion: Small molecules like Urea, Glucose, and Water ($< 4\text{ nm}$) easily slip through the fenestrations and slit diaphragms.
Charge Repulsion: Proteins (like Albumin) are negatively charged. The Basement Membrane is lined with polyanions (heparan sulfate) that repel them like magnets, keeping them in the blood.
