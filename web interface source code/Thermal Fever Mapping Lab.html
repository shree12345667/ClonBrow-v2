Hemodynamic Heat Transfer

Q = c · F · (Tc - Ts)

Blood has a high specific heat capacity ($c$). By adjusting the flow rate ($F$) via vasodilation or vasoconstriction, the hypothalamus controls how much internal heat is carried from the core ($T_c$) to the skin ($T_s$).

Fever (Chills Phase): The hypothalamus raises the set-point. Vessels constrict (low blood flow), making the skin feel cold and reducing heat loss to rapidly build up core heat.
Fever (Flush Phase): The fever breaks. Vessels dilate massively, turning the skin red and hot to rapidly dump excess heat into the environment via convection and sweat.
